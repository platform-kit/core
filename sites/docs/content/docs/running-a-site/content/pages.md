A **landing page** is a route (`/`, `/pricing`, `/about`, or any path you choose) and an ordered list of **sections**. Content → Pages lists them; one opens in the page builder.

## The page builder

The main area is the page as a stack of sections, each a block: a small sketch of its layout, its name, what it holds ("6 features", "3 columns") and the first line of what it says. Each section is one kind — the catalogue below, and whatever a site adds of its own — and each kind has its own fields.

- **Add a section** opens a gallery of the kinds — a sketch and a line each, grouped (openers, content, proof, commerce, people, what draws on the site's own data, closers, the site's own), searched from the top, chosen by a click or Enter — and seeds the new section with a plain example. The button at the foot adds at the end; the **+** on the seam between two blocks adds there. A section with **presets** shows them at the top of its form ("Start from"): a ready shape laid over the fields.
- **Drag** a block by its handle to reorder; the arrows move it a step; **duplicate** and **remove** are on every block.
- Open a block to edit its fields as a form: a line, a paragraph, a switch, a choice as a row of buttons, a picture as a url, an upload or one chosen from the [library](/content/docs/running-a-site/content/media), a glyph from a palette (the theme's and Lucide's, searched), a list of lines, a list of records (each a row that opens, with a title of its own, dragged into order, duplicated) — every field labelled in plain words, what is wrong said under the field. Nothing is typed as a grammar.
- **Live preview** draws the page beside the builder as you edit. Opening a block scrolls the preview to that section and lights it for a moment; clicking a section in the preview opens its block. On a site with more than one look, a switch flips between looks without saving.

You never see or type JSON or YAML; the file is written for you and validated on save.

## The page's own fields

Above the sections: the page's **title** (internal), its **route**, an optional **SEO title** (the browser tab; blank means the site's name), the **description** for search and sharing, and an optional **share image**.

## The catalogue

Every section is generic — drawn in the site's look, with nothing of any one site's meaning in it — and every word it shows is the page's own. The few words a section has of its own (Next, Previous, Close, Show, the email field's states, _Step n_, _Read_, the countdown's units, _All_ on a gallery's filters, _Add to bag_) are the `sections` scope under UI strings. Many have **layouts**, and a few offer **presets** at the top of their form — a ready shape laid over the fields, to start from.

### The look of a section

Every section but the Marquee, the Close, the Divider and the Announcement takes the same fold of look fields, under **Section look** at the foot of its form — so any section can be the dark band, the glowing one, the one that arrives with a blur:

- **Tone** — _paper_ (the ground), _wash_ (a faint band), _accent_, _ink_ (inverted: the look's ink as the ground; the eyebrow and the buttons swap to suit).
- **Glow** — none, a bloom under the cards as the pointer passes (_cards_), a halo behind the visual (_visual_), a light at the foot (_foot_) or in a corner (_corner_).
- **Ground** — none, _grain_, _dots_, _grid_, a _gradient_ from the look's light, or an _aurora_ that drifts.
- **Spacing** — tight, normal or roomy; **width** — narrow (a reading measure), normal, or full bleed.
- **Reveal** — how its pictures arrive as it scrolls into view: at once, a _fade_, a _blur_ that sharpens, a _zoom_ that settles, or _tiles_ wiped in.
- **Anchor** — an id, so a link can go to it (`/#pricing`).

A page may also set `progress: true` for a thin reading-progress bar along its top. In any string, `**bold**`, `*italic*` (set in the look's serif), `[a link](/app)` and `^^gradient^^` (the accent running to its light) are drawn.

The **site footer** is chrome and the layout draws it on every page from `content/site.md` (Settings → Site settings), so no page has to remember it. A page ends without one by saying `footer: false` — which the landing does, because its close is the ground the whole page has been walking toward and a column of links under it would be an anticlimax. A page that wants a different footer, or one in a different place, puts the **Footer** section in its own sections and the layout draws none.

A short page still fills the screen: the shell is a column at least a viewport tall and the footer takes whatever is left, so the leftover room on a tall monitor is the foot of the page in the foot's own colour rather than a band of nothing between two things.
| **Form** | One of the site's forms, inline on the page — chosen by name, under an eyebrow, heading and lead of the page's own (the forms module; see [Forms](/content/docs/running-a-site/marketing/forms)) | — |
| **Entries** | A content model's entries — the model, cards or a list, how many (0 for all) — each linking to its page when the model has pages (the models module; see [Content models](/content/docs/running-a-site/content/content-models)) | — |

**Glyphs.** Wherever a section takes a glyph (a feature, a perk, a step, a tab, a pricing rung), the form offers a palette: the theme's own twenty and the whole Lucide set, searched by name. The build ships only the glyphs the pages name.

**An email captured on a page** — the Newsletter, a CTA's field, the hero's, the footer's — is a response to one of the site's forms: name a form that has an email field, and the address lands in Forms → Responses beside every other answer, with the form's own delivery (an email, a webhook) running as it does for any response. A person who is already on the list is told so, not refused.

The demo site shows every one of these at `/sections`, and a commerce page built from the store's sections at `/shop`. A look may draw a section differently from its base (a **skin** — the marquee as a running head, the path as a ridge into the sun) without the page knowing: the page names the section once and never changes with the look.

## Experiments on a page

Two pages on one route are an A/B test. The pages know nothing about it; an **experiment** record (Marketing → Experiments) points at them and decides who sees which. See [Ads and experiments](/content/docs/running-a-site/marketing/ads-and-experiments).

## The close at the foot of other pages

The landing's Close section ends every article and the store's home too, worded by an **ad** in the footer placement when there is one, else by the site's own words (`article.close` under UI strings → library). A landing page keeps its own Close as a section.
