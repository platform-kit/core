# Noise

Begin by noticing how much of the day is noise — not sound, but claim. Every screen claims you; every notification is a small hand on your sleeve. By evening you have been tugged a thousand times, and you feel it as a tiredness you cannot name, because nothing tiring happened. ==The day was not hard; it was loud.==

Noise is not the same as information. Most of what claims you is not something you needed to know but something that needed you to look. The distinction matters, because you can refuse a claim without refusing knowledge. The message will still be there in an hour. The world will not.

Try counting, for one afternoon, how many times something asks for your attention that you did not go looking for. Do not change anything; just count. The number is usually in the hundreds. ==You cannot repair what you have not yet noticed being spent.=={sun}

# The door

The quiet room has a door, and the door is a decision made once rather than a hundred times. You do not decide, each morning, whether to have ten quiet minutes; you decided that already, the way you decided where you live. ==A decision made once is a door; a decision made daily is a draught.==

Choose the time and the place and let them be dull. The kitchen chair after the coffee. The bench by the bus stop. The corner of the bed before you get up. Dullness is a feature: an exciting place invites you to do something exciting in it, and the point of the room is that nothing happens there.

Then close the door. The phone goes in another room — not face down, not on silent, but away, where it cannot be reached without standing. This is the one hard rule, and it is hard only for a week. ==Distance, not willpower, is what keeps the door shut.==

# Ten minutes of nothing

What happens in the room is nothing, and nothing is harder than it sounds. For the first two minutes the mind runs through the day like a dog let off a lead. Let it. ==You are not trying to stop the mind; you are trying to stop feeding it.==

Sit, or walk slowly, or look out of a window. Do not read, do not listen, do not plan. When a thought arrives, notice that it has arrived and let it go by, the way you would let a stranger pass on a path. It will come back. Let it pass again.

Around the fifth minute something loosens. The thoughts come more slowly and with more space between them, and in the space you can hear — for the first time that day — whatever it is you actually think about things. This is not mystical. It is what a mind sounds like when nobody is talking to it.

Ten minutes is enough. Longer is fine but not necessary, and a rule that says twenty will be broken by a day that has only ten. ==The room is ten minutes long because ten minutes is a room you can enter every day.=={sun}

# What comes back

Leave the room and the day is still there, as loud as before. But you are not the same in it. For an hour or two the claims land differently: you see them arrive, and seeing them arrive is most of the way to choosing which to answer. ==Attention that has been rested can tell the difference between a claim and a need.==

Over weeks the hour or two grows. People who keep the room describe the same thing in different words: the day gets wider. There is more room in it, not because anything was removed, but because less of it is spent being tugged.

What comes back, in the end, is not calm — calm comes and goes — but choice. The room gives you back the ability to decide what your attention is for, and that is the only ability that the loud day takes. ==Everything else you can do with a torn attention; deciding is the one thing you cannot.=={sun}
