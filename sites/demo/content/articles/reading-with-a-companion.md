A companion is a second file beside the book: for every chapter, a summary that loses no important point, a list of the ideas to keep, and five questions to check you kept them.

The reader shows the summary in place of the chapter when you ask, and the quiz at the end of it. Pass four of five and the chapter is yours — bronze for the first, silver for half the book, gold for all of it.

## Why a summary is not a shortcut

A summary written by the author's editor is not the book made smaller; it is the book made findable. Read the summary first and the chapter reads faster, because you know where it is going. Read it after and you find out what you missed. Either way you end up having read the chapter twice, which is the only way anyone has ever remembered a book.

## Why a quiz

Because recognising an idea is not the same as having it. Five questions, one right answer each, no trick questions — just the moment where you find out whether the chapter stuck. It usually did. When it did not, the summary is one tap away.
